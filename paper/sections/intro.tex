\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Atmospheric muons penetrate building materials and carry information about the matter along their trajectories. Their attenuation has revealed hidden structures in archaeological and geological objects \citep{alvarez1970,morishima2017,procureur2018,bonechi2020}. Inside a room, the same particles can image the slab and beams overhead without an artificial radiation source.

The challenge is geometric: one tracker position supplies an angular projection, not a unique three-dimensional image. Moving the tracker adds parallax, while two views still leave weakly constrained vertical structure. We examine this practical limit using the cafeteria ceiling at Tel Aviv University. Transmission maps, a voxel reconstruction and independent beam-shadow diagnostics establish the lateral structure; a geometric model with fitted beam opacity estimates depth. On-site dimensions are comparison values, not fit targets.
