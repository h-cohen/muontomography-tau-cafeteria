\section{Three-dimensional room reconstruction}\label{sec:volume}
The three-dimensional room image is a principal result. It combines response-corrected projections, fitted detector geometry and cross-position constraints in one voxel field, revealing repeated elongated ceiling structures beyond individual angular shadows or height estimates.

The joint reconstruction used \ReconstructionRows{} angular rows and \ReconstructionVoxels{} voxels, with residual statistic $\ReconstructionChisqPerRow$ per row. Figure~\ref{fig:volume} emphasizes the beams and their spatial arrangement. The shared-view check supports lateral placement; the preceding uncertainty analysis qualifies inferred dimensions. The field represents effective opacity, with vertical extent less constrained than lateral structure.


The beam-minus-between-beam voxel profile has half-maximum extent $\BeamdepthZprofileFwhm\,\mathrm{m}$, with crossings at $\BeamdepthZprofileBottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\BeamdepthZprofileTop\,\mathrm{m}$. This exceeds the conditional box depth, but the distributed inverse field and the parametric angular fit need not have the same extent. The discrepancy may reflect finite angular/aperture resolution, the two-view null space, reconstruction choices or box-model assumptions; display thresholds further change visible extent. Vertical smoothing is disabled but does not remove that ambiguity. The analytic feature-height scale $\BeamdepthZprofileResolution\,\mathrm{m}$ is not a measured point-spread kernel for deconvolving this profile. Neither its FWHM nor the number of visible voxel layers is a physical beam-depth measurement.
