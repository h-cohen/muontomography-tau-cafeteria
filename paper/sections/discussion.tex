\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Moving one instrument introduced parallax without requiring a second tracker. The two angular views identified repeated lateral beam structure and supported several complementary height estimators. Their geometric information is nevertheless limited: a single-view ray can redistribute opacity along its path, and a two-view inverse problem retains many volumes with similar projections. Coverage-aware display and raw-data beam estimates are therefore necessary to avoid interpreting regularization as a measurement.

The measured beam bottom and depth were reported separately and were not constraints in the fitting objective. The fitted-versus-measured comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:depth} is a scientific result of the chosen estimator. It should not be promoted to a statement that the building differs from the on-site measurement: the finite aperture, background approximation, free-baseline scale assumptions, fit-weight estimation and optimizer modes can all contribute. The on-site measurements have no supplied error bars, so their differences cannot be assigned a formal significance from the fit budget alone.

Fixing concrete density removes a large opacity--path-length freedom, but makes the depth estimate conditional on muon transmission physics and inferred overburden. The range-out model neglects straggling and angular migration, while the scattering calculation is a limited perturbation model. The sea-level spectrum alternative and density shifts explore part of this uncertainty. They do not characterize every deviation of reinforced concrete, detector threshold or overhead background from the adopted model. A low nominal chi-square, if obtained, would not by itself establish a unique depth; a high value would indicate that quoted parameter precision should be interpreted cautiously.

A surveyed relative pose, including separate offsets across and along the beams, would provide a stronger scale anchor than self-calibration with a free baseline. Additional views with transverse separation would better distinguish beam bottom, top and width, and reduce reliance on a common-box model. Independent tests with nonuniform slabs, room edges and sub-ray transmission averaging would assess real-model discrepancy beyond the matching-model phantom recoveries reported here.
