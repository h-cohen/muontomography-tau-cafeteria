\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Moving a single tracker supplied enough parallax to image repeated ceiling structure and estimate its height. It did not uniquely determine vertical beam extent. The discrepancy with on-site dimensions is a finding of this estimator, not evidence that the building differs from the measurement.

Depth depends on the beam cross-section, statistical weights and inferred background. Fitting effective beam opacities avoids fixing concrete transmission, but leaves contrast and depth partly interchangeable; background shifts cover only part of model uncertainty. Free-baseline calibration also retains a scale assumption, and the measured and fitted height origins were not independently registered. Known-geometry phantom recovery therefore cannot establish real-ceiling accuracy.

A surveyed pose, beam cross-section measurements and additional transverse views would better separate bottom, top and width. Independent tests of sub-ray flux averaging and nonuniform backgrounds are needed to distinguish geometric depth from a material-model effect.
