\section{Cross-position autofocus}\label{sec:autofocus}
At each trial height, a thin layer model was fitted to one view and scored on the other, with the positions exchanged for the second score. The held-out residual offset was removed and the largest residuals were trimmed per view. The candidate height with the smallest averaged score was refined locally by a parabola. The layer lattice had spacing $\InputsFocusSpacing\,\mathrm{m}$; its half-voxel-shifted twin was evaluated as well. This averaging reduced the dependence of the focus minimum on the phase of a lattice relative to the beam pattern.

The nominal autofocus height was $\AutofocusZ\,\mathrm{m}$, with count-bootstrap standard deviation $\UncertaintyAutofocusZStat\,\mathrm{m}$ and evaluated total uncertainty $\UncertaintyAutofocusZTotal\,\mathrm{m}$. Synthetic ceilings were generated using the fitted footprint, material model and campaign geometry, then processed by the same autofocus routine. The signed mean recovery bias was $\ValidationFocusBias\,\mathrm{m}$ and the maximum absolute recovery error was $\ValidationFocusMaxError\,\mathrm{m}$. The injected reference is the beam-layer centre, rather than its bottom face. Consequently, autofocus and the box-fit bottom height need not coincide even for a well-modelled ceiling. These matching-model tests quantify recovery under the specified assumptions; they do not exclude bias from an unmodelled wall or background edge.
