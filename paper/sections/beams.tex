\section{Ceiling height}\label{sec:beams}
These supporting diagnostics locate an effective ceiling plane. Here height is its vertical position relative to the detector reference, not beam thickness $h=z_{\rm top}-z_{\rm bottom}$.

\emph{Autofocus and triangulation.} A thin-layer cross-position prediction scan gives $\AutofocusZ\pm\UncertaintyAutofocusZTotal\,\mathrm{m}$, averaged over two half-voxel-shifted grids. Independently matched angular shadows obey $s_{x,p,k}=(X_k-x_p)/(z-z_p)$; their common transverse height is $z_x=\BeamsZX\pm\UncertaintyBeamsZxTotal\,\mathrm{m}$, with pitch $\BeamsPitch\,\mathrm{m}$.

The estimates agree within their evaluated budgets but share the data and fitted pose, so they are not independent scale checks. They localize effective shadow features rather than the bottom face. Comparison with the on-site bottom $\InputsMeasuredBeamBottom\,\mathrm{m}$ also requires height registration; agreement with the building is not established by the quoted errors alone.

As a check of the image, we took the voxel layer at the triangulated height and located the beams in it. Where both positions see the ceiling, they match the beam positions found directly in the opacity maps with a mean offset of $\BeamsGateMeanAbsOffset\,\mathrm{m}$. This is a shared-data lateral check, not independent depth or scale.
