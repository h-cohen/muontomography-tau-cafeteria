\section{Beam depth}\label{sec:depth}
A common rectangular-box model described bottom height $z_0$, width $w$, depth $h$ and transverse beam coordinates. It was fitted directly to the opacity maps, independently of voxel regularization:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_i=\sum_k\kappa_k L_{ik}(z_0,w,h,x_k)+b_i,
\end{equation}
where $L_{ik}$ is the aperture-averaged path through beam $k$ and $b_i$ is a smooth per-position polynomial background. Nonnegative beam opacities $\kappa_k$ and background coefficients were projected out at each geometry step. For beams spanning the full accepted footprint in $y$, path averages were integrated analytically; finite-end crossings retained numerical quadrature. Multiple starts shared the same parameter bounds. Material density was not imposed, and measured dimensions were used only for comparison.

The optimizer reached a thin-depth bound and the profile minimum lay at the shallow edge (Fig.~\ref{fig:depth}); consequently no physical bottom or depth measurement is reported. The on-site values were $\InputsMeasuredBeamBottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\InputsMeasuredBeamDepth\,\mathrm{m}$. The conditional optimizer parameters were bottom $\BeamdepthZbottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h=\BeamdepthH\,\mathrm{m}$, with width $\BeamdepthW\,\mathrm{m}$ and residual statistic $\BeamdepthChisqPerDof$ per nominal degree of freedom. These numbers describe the fitted thin-layer approximation, not recovered beam dimensions.

The profile remains nearly flat toward thin layers, where increasing opacity compensates for decreasing depth. Analytic count weights also produced a bound fit. Beam cross-section, angular response and background misspecification can introduce further bias; agreement with a survey value was not an optimization criterion.

On box-and-slab phantoms retaining the campaign's normal column contrast, the same estimator had maximum mean depth bias $\ValidationDepthMaxBias\,\mathrm{m}$. This checks numerical recovery within the assumed model rather than the accuracy of the real-room dimensions.
