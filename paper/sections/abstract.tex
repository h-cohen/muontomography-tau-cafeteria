Muon transmission measurements from a small tracker provide angular projections of an overhead structure, but recovering its vertical geometry from a limited number of viewpoints is an underdetermined problem. We used one scintillator tracker at two floor positions beneath the Tel Aviv University cafeteria ceiling, together with an open-sky reference exposure. Live-time normalization defined an absolute opacity scale. An aperture-aware voxel reconstruction, cross-position autofocus, and beam-peak triangulation were combined with a rectangular-beam fit whose opacity was pinned to concrete transmission physics. The box fit recovered a beam bottom of $\BeamdepthZbottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and vertical depth of $\BeamdepthH\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with the on-site measurements of $\InputsMeasuredBeamBottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\InputsMeasuredBeamDepth\,\mathrm{m}$, respectively. Their statistical uncertainties were $\UncertaintyDepthZbottomStat\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\UncertaintyDepthHStat\,\mathrm{m}$; combined uncertainties within the evaluated error budget were $\UncertaintyDepthZbottomTotal\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\UncertaintyDepthHTotal\,\mathrm{m}$. These estimates are conditional on the self-calibrated pose and the concrete model. The measured heights were not imposed on the fit. We report the discrepancy, synthetic recovery tests, residual goodness of fit, and a coverage-aware voxel visualization, distinguishing identifiable lateral structure from weakly constrained vertical extent.
