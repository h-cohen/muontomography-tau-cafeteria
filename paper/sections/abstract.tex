Cosmic muons provide a passive probe of structures hidden overhead. We imaged a real room's ceiling using one scintillator tracker at two floor positions and an open-sky reference. Response-aware transmission maps, voxel reconstruction and beam-shadow parallax revealed the repeated ceiling structure. Transverse triangulation placed the beam-opacity plane at $\BeamsZX\,\mathrm{m}$, with evaluated uncertainty budget $\UncertaintyBeamsZxTotal\,\mathrm{m}$. An expanded-array forward fit gave exploratory conditional depth $\ArraydepthH\,\mathrm{m}$, with count spread $\ArraydepthHSigma\,\mathrm{m}$ under fixed relative beam-centre assumptions. A more flexible matched-beam model favoured thin layers; background and array assumptions limited interpretation of vertical extent. On-site bottom and depth measurements of $\InputsMeasuredBeamBottom\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\InputsMeasuredBeamDepth\,\mathrm{m}$ remained independent comparisons. Profile refits and count bootstrap exposed the opacity--depth ambiguity. The room reconstruction demonstrates passive imaging in a real room from two positions while separating visible structure from dimensions the data do not resolve.
